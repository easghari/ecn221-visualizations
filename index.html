ECN 221 Business Statistics — Interactive Visualizations

Instructor: Ehsan Asghari-Ghara · W. P. Carey School of Business, Arizona State University

  • Classical vs. Relative Frequency Probability: Rolling Two Dice

    Count the equally likely outcomes of two dice, then roll them thousands of times and watch the relative frequency settle onto the classical probability.

  • Probability Relationships: Complements, Unions, Conditional Probability and Independence

    See complements, the addition rule, conditional probability and independence on 100 customers, a Venn diagram and a joint probability table at once.

  • Binomial vs. Hypergeometric: Sampling With and Without Replacement

    Watch two inspectors sample the same shipment, one putting parts back and one setting them aside, then compare the binomial and hypergeometric distributions and the 5% rule.

  • Continuous Distributions: Probability as Area

    Drag an interval under a normal density and watch the probability fill in; see why every normal is the standard normal in disguise; check the empirical rule by simulation.

  • Central Limit Theorem

    Watch the sampling distribution of the sample mean build up one sample at a time, and compare it with the normal curve the CLT predicts.

  • Confidence Intervals

    Build one confidence interval per sample, see which ones capture the true mean, and check whether a 95% confidence level really means 95% of the time.

  • Hypothesis Tests: Interpreting α and Power

    Run the same test on thousands of samples: see α as the false-alarm rate when H₀ is true, then move the true mean and watch power build.

  • Comparing Two Means: Is the Gap Real?

    Two independent samples never have the same mean. See how the standard error of the difference decides whether a gap is real, and compare z, pooled t and Welch t.

  • Paired vs. Independent Samples

    Run the paired and the independent test on the same before/after data and watch pairing cancel person-to-person variation.

  • Simple Regression: From the Population to the Sample

    Rotate the joint density of (X, Y) in 3D, see where the population regression line comes from, then draw samples and watch the fitted slope's sampling distribution build.
